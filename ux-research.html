Products evaluated

Research across the healthcare digital experience.

Patient and Caregiver Products

For patient portal onboarding, scheduling, and intake, study whether people can find the next step, understand what is required, and complete the process. Test terminology, instructions, and mobile interactions with the intended audience.

Clinical and Professional Tools

For clinical dashboards and EHR-connected workflows, examine how professionals find information and hand work to the next person. Clinical decision-support interfaces require a separate assessment of intended use and safety-related tasks.

Staff and Administrative Systems

Follow the work across referrals, enrollment, recurring tasks, documentation, and billing preparation. Map ownership and missing information so a product recommendation addresses the whole handoff, not just one screen.

Concepts and Prototypes

Use concept reviews, wireframes, or interactive prototypes to test the riskiest assumptions before building. For live products, pair observed task behavior with product analytics when the data and consent support it.

Methods

Choose the method that matches the product question.

The approach can be exploratory, evaluative, quantitative, or a focused combination.

Foundational and evaluative

  • Discovery and user interviews
  • Contextual inquiry and field studies
  • Diary studies and workflow research
  • Mental models and jobs-to-be-done
  • Journey maps and service blueprints
  • Concept, feature, and preference testing
  • Value-proposition and desirability testing

Usability and information architecture

  • Moderated and unmoderated usability tests
  • Remote, in-person, mobile, and cross-device tests
  • Task-based and think-aloud testing
  • Onboarding and form testing
  • Open, closed, and hybrid card sorting
  • Tree testing and first-click testing
  • Navigation, findability, and taxonomy testing

Specialized evaluation

Test clarity, accessibility, and measurable performance.

Content and Comprehension

Check whether users can explain an instruction in their own words and act on it. Health literacy research can cover medical terminology, consent content, error messages, and the difference between understanding a claim and trusting it.

Expert Review

Heuristic evaluation, cognitive walkthrough, accessibility review, inclusive design, UX audit, and content audit.

UX Benchmarking

Define the task and success criteria before measuring completion, time, or errors. SUS and UMUX-Lite summarize perceived usability; SEQ captures perceived ease after a task. Scores need a consistent study design and context to support comparisons.

Behavioral Analytics

Funnels, conversion, clickstream, heatmaps, session recordings, retention, and A/B testing.

Digital UX and medical device human factors

Match the evaluation to the risk of the task.

A confusing portal sign-up and an error during medical device use call for different research designs. Digital UX research can examine findability, comprehension, task completion, and workflow fit. A medical device human factors program also needs to consider intended users, use environments, use-related risks, and safety-critical tasks.

Formal human factors validation or work under IEC 62366-1 requires a separately defined scope and appropriate regulatory expertise. An exploratory workflow study does not establish device safety or satisfy a regulatory submission. We clarify that boundary before agreeing on the research plan.

Questions before a project

Healthcare UX research, scoped clearly.

How is participant privacy handled?

We agree on consent, recording permissions, access, retention, and reporting before recruitment. Simulated or appropriately de-identified environments are preferred where they answer the question. If protected health information is required, HIPAA applicability, agreements, and the data environment must be confirmed before the study begins. Do not send patient-identifiable information through the quote form.

Can the research include healthcare professionals and staff?

Yes. The audience is defined by the work: physicians, nurses, caregivers, patients, administrative staff, or other roles relevant to the product. Recruitment criteria, specialist access, study location, and feasibility are agreed during scoping.

Which usability metrics should a study use?

Task completion and observed errors help evaluate whether a workflow works. Time on task describes efficiency under the study conditions. SEQ, SUS, or UMUX-Lite can add perceived ease or usability. We choose the measures after defining the decision and tasks, rather than collecting every score by default.

Can you evaluate an early concept before it has users?

Yes. Workflow mapping, expert review, and prototype research can identify assumptions to test before launch. Those findings are separated from evidence collected with participants. The HealthyFlow case illustrates product and workflow definition; user validation remains a subsequent step.

Is this the same as medical device human factors validation?

No. Exploratory UX research and formal medical device validation have different purposes and evidence requirements. Safety-related intended use, critical tasks, regulatory expectations, and specialist responsibilities must be defined separately.
