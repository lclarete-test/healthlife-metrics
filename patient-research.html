How do patient and caregiver journeys differ?

A patient journey describes the person's experience of care. A caregiver journey can include finding information, arranging appointments, coordinating family responsibilities, and seeking support. We keep those perspectives distinct and connect them where the decision requires it.

When is digital ethnography useful?

It can reveal the language people use, recurring questions, and the context of online conversations. It is useful for discovery and shaping a research brief. It does not establish population prevalence or replace interviews when the question requires direct exploration with patients and caregivers.

How are sensitive experiences handled?

Recruitment, consent, recording permissions, confidentiality, and reporting are agreed during scoping. Online material requires its own assessment of access, context, and identification risk. Portfolio examples use aggregated findings without identifiable participant posts. Do not submit patient-identifiable information through the quote form.
